Recap

Two research questions

RQ1 · AS PROPOSED

Residual Koopman MPC

Can a Koopman (EDMDc) model of only the mismatch, built on the known spinning-equilibrium model of Mueller & D'Andrea, predict and control better than the physics model alone or a full-state Koopman model?

RQ2 · ADDED AFTER THE PROPOSAL

Certified detection-delay budget

A fault detector needs 30–130 ms to notice a dead rotor. What is the largest delay for which recovery is guaranteed, and how do the RQ1 controllers change it?

The link: each capture controller (LQR, analytical MPC, residual Koopman MPC) is compared by the detection delay it can tolerate.

RQ1 is the question from the proposal we presented: build the known physics of the spinning, failed vehicle into the model, and let a Koopman model learn only what the physics gets wrong. RQ2 is new. While reviewing the literature we found that every recovery method assumes the failure is detected instantly, but real detectors take tens to hundreds of milliseconds. RQ2 asks for a guaranteed bound on that delay, using Lyapunov and LMI tools from this course. The two connect: whichever controller catches the vehicle after detection, we can compare them by the delay they tolerate. Nothing from the proposal was dropped.
