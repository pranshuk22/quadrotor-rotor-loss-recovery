RQ2 · Why delay matters

Before detection, nothing stops the roll

Rotor 2 cannot go below its idle thrust (0.2 N), so the roll acceleration is at least l·f_min / I_xx = 10.6 rad/s² for any healthy-controller tuning. Tilt reaches about 13° at 100 ms and 37° at 200 ms.

Why does the delay matter so much? When rotor 4 dies, rotor 2 on the opposite side is no longer balanced, which creates a roll torque. The healthy controller reacts by cutting rotor 2, but a running motor cannot go below its idle thrust. So a roll acceleration of at least 10.6 radians per second squared remains, whatever the tuning. The plot shows three tunings, all above this lower bound. After 100 milliseconds the vehicle is already tilted about 13 degrees, and after 200 about 37.
