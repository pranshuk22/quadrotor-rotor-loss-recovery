To turn the delay question into a guarantee, we need a certified set of states from which the failure controller always recovers despite thrust limits. Our first version uses the Hu and Lin saturation conditions as LMIs on the linear model at the spinning equilibrium. It certifies about 22 percent of the way from the equilibrium to the state right after a failure in hover; simulation shows recovery up to about 70 percent, so it is about three times conservative. The slice shows why: the controller only fails when the spin rate is near zero, but a certificate built at the full spin cannot reach there. That is exactly the gap our next step addresses, by treating the yaw rate as a varying parameter and certifying over the whole spin-up. These are certificates for the linear model; the nonlinear gap is measured by simulation.
