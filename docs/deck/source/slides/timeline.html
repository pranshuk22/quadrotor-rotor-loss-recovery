When one rotor dies, a quadrotor cannot hover level any more. Mueller and D'Andrea showed it can still fly by spinning steadily about a tilted axis, called the relaxed hover. But there is a gap in time: the fault detector needs some time tau to notice the failure. During that time the normal controller keeps flying a vehicle that has lost a rotor. Only after detection does the failure controller take over and try to capture the vehicle into the spinning state. Most guarantee-based papers assume tau equals zero. Başak and Prempain proved stability for a linear model with a fixed delay, but without thrust limits and without finding the largest tolerable delay. Fast detectors take 30 to 130 milliseconds.
