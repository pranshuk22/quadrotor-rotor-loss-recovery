\subsection{What the results mean}
\paragraph{Physics first, learning second.} The physics model of Mueller and D'Andrea, which we reproduced to every printed digit except in one internally inconsistent published case, already describes the spinning drone well: it gives the right equilibrium, the right flight spin-up time and a controller that works. Learning helps only in the second order, and only when the learned part is restricted to the error. Learning everything from data produced an unstable model, as our proposal predicted.

\paragraph{A calibration, not yet a general model.} The learned residual is accurate on the plant it saw and harmful on a different plant. As it stands it behaves like an offline calibration of one vehicle. Online adaptation, or a residual that also uses a short history of states, is needed before it can be called a general correction for rotor failure.

\paragraph{Integral action is the best bias remedy tested.} Among all controllers of E7 and E8, the LQR with an integrator is the best trade-off: it removes the steady height error (at most 0.024\,m) and leaves the capture performance of the LQR unchanged, and it beats the residual Koopman MPC on both counts. The residual is therefore not the best way to remove the bias; its remaining value, if any, lies in transients or in effects that integral action cannot see, which we did not test.

\paragraph{Learning the model error: what survived.} The residual model is accurate and data-efficient on the plant it was trained on, and it removes part of a steady error inside a convex MPC. Everything we added to make it more general or more capable did not change that picture in a useful way: online adaptation repairs the worst held-out case but not the prediction on the training plant, training on transients makes the capture worse, a longer horizon or a heavier attitude weight changes the capture by at most one delay, and noisy training data are harmful. For the practical goal (a small steady error and a safe capture) a classical integrator is better than any learned variant tested.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Spin-up thrust.} The capture experiments use the spin-up thrust of 2.95\,N, which was read from a plot of the flight data and carries some uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Nominal plant for the capture tests.} The capture tests of the first experiments use the nominal plant or family A; the published models over-predict the steady spin rate in flight by about 20 to 27\%.
  \item \textbf{Generalization.} The residual model helps reliably only on the plant family it was trained on.
  \item \textbf{Truth-plant realism.} Families A and B add thrust loss, inertia error and motor lag; families A$^+$ and B$^+$ also add air-relative thrust and quadratic drag (Section~\ref{sec:realistic}). There is no translational drag, and sensor noise was studied only for the residual and the MPC (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
  \item \textbf{Simulation only.} No result has been tested on hardware.
  \item \textbf{Full-state measurement.} All controllers are given the exact state. A real spinning drone needs an estimator for the tilt, height and vertical speed, and sensor noise will affect both the learned residual and the MPC.
  \item \textbf{Closed-loop data.} The learning data are recorded under LQR feedback with added excitation, which correlates states and inputs; possible bias from this is not analysed.
  \item \textbf{Missing baselines.} Grey-box estimation of the few uncertain physical parameters (thrust loss, inertias, motor time constant) is the obvious model-based alternative to a black-box residual; it has not been compared. The offset-free baselines of Section~\ref{sec:offsetfree} were compared on one start per plant.
  \item \textbf{Literature access.} Several closely related papers~\cite{stephan2018lpv,lanzon2014flight} were not available to us in an open version and have not been read in full; our statements about them rely on abstracts and citing papers, and our novelty statements are the outcome of a search.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future work}
The following items are not part of the results above.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Offset-free action that survives the capture:} the state-model offset-free MPC fails the capture after the hand-over (Section~\ref{sec:offsetfree}); switching the estimator on only after the capture is an untested suggestion.
  \item \textbf{Grey-box estimation} of the few uncertain physical parameters (thrust loss, inertias, motor time constant) as the model-based alternative to a black-box residual, and a correction for the bias of fits on noisy data (for example total least squares).
  \item \textbf{Output feedback:} an estimator for the tilt, height and vertical speed from realistic sensors, and its effect on the learned residual.
  \item \textbf{Guarantees} for a learned controller of the failed vehicle, which would need a bound on the true plant mismatch and an argument for the MPC constraints.
  \item \textbf{Hardware:} nothing here has been tested on a real drone.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusion}
We built a simulator of a quadrotor with complete rotor loss and showed that it reproduces three published papers and the spin-up time of a real flight. On the plant it was trained on, a Koopman model that learns only the error of the physics model is the most accurate predictor from 100\,ms to 1\,s, needs less data than a Koopman model of the full dynamics, and reduces a steady altitude error by one third inside a convex MPC. It does not carry over to a plant with different parameters, and online adaptation helps only partly. For removing a steady error an LQR with an integrator is better than every learned variant we tested, and the MPC on the full-state Koopman model fails the capture earlier than the others. Training the residual on capture transients, changing the MPC horizon or weights, and varying the dictionary, regularization or sampling period did not improve the capture, a residual trained on noisy measurements is harmful, and all of this holds on truth plants with air-relative thrust and quadratic drag.
